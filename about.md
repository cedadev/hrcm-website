#### The High-Resolution Climate Modelling group is a long-standing collaboration between scientists from UK institutions at the frontier of exploiting weather-resolving models to simulate the building blocks of Earth's climate—the National Centre for Atmospheric Science, University of Reading, and Met Office Hadley Centre for Climate Science and Services, using high-resolution global climate models to advance our the understanding and prediction of weather and climate.

Over more than a decade of collaborative research, the group has helped demonstrate how increasing model resolution improves the representation of important climate processes, including convection, synoptic cyclones, atmospheric circulation, ocean–atmosphere interactions, and extreme precipitation. Through national and international projects, including [HiGEM](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/climate/climate-modelling), [UPSCALE](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/climate/climate-modelling), [PRIMAVERA](https://www.primavera-h2020.eu/), and HRCM has contributed to a growing body of evidence on the benefits of high-resolution climate simulations, as well as the challenges that remain in translating model fidelity into more reliable climate projections.

HRCM's research has shown that finer resolution can better represent the small-scale processes that shape regional weather and climate extremes, while also revealing the importance of model biases, ocean resolution and interactions between different parts of the climate system. Through continued collaboration, shared modelling experiments and peer-reviewed publications, HRCM advances the scientific basis for understanding future climate change and developing more useful regional climate information for research, adaptation and decision-making.

Explore the group's [publications](https://hrcm.ceda.ac.uk/publications/) to discover the research underpinning these developments.

<p>The Met Office Hadley Centre is one of the world's foremost climate change research centres, and the UK's primary source of climate change information. Our aim is to provide climate science and services to people and organisations, so they can make better decisions to stay safe and thrive. We do this by working with partners around the globe, carrying out world leading research to monitor changes in the climate, understand and determine the cause of these changes, develop state-of-the-art computer models for climate predictions and projections, serve the needs of UK government, and develop services with end-users. Since our foundation in 1990, the Met Office Hadley Centre has been recognised as a global partner of choice for climate science and services. Hadley Centre scientists have contributed to all Assessment Reports by the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) and produced more than 2,200 scientific peer-reviewed articles since 1990.</p>

<p>The National Centre for Atmospheric Science (NCAS) is a world leader in atmospheric science. We carry out research programmes on:</p>

<ul>
<li>The science of climate change, including modelling and predictions</li>
<li>Atmospheric composition, including air quality</li>
<li>Weather, including hazardous weather</li>
<li>Technologies for observing and modelling the atmosphere</li>
</ul>

<p>We provide scientific facilities for researchers across the UK to enable coordinated, national-scale atmospheric science. These include a world-leading research aircraft, ground-based instrumentation pool, access to computer models and facilities for storing, accessing and analysing data. In short, we provide the UK academic community and <strong><a href="http://www.nerc.ac.uk">NERC</a></strong>, our parent organisation, with national capability in atmospheric science. We communicate our findings and knowledge for the benefit of policy-makers, other scientists, and the general public.</p>
