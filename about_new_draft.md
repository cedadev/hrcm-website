
### About HRCM

The **High-Resolution Climate Modelling (HRCM) programme** brings together climate scientists from the National Centre for Atmospheric Science (NCAS), the University of Reading and the Met Office to advance the understanding and prediction of the climate system using high-resolution global climate models.

By increasing model resolution, HRCM research aims to represent atmospheric and oceanic processes in greater detail, improve the simulation of regional climate and extreme weather, and better understand how these processes may change in a warming climate.

Find out more about our [research](/research/), the people involved in the programme on our [People page](/people/), and our [publications](/publications/).

#### Why high-resolution climate modelling?

Many important climate processes occur at relatively small spatial scales. These include tropical cyclones, intense rainfall, atmospheric circulation features and interactions between the atmosphere and ocean.

Global climate models with finer resolution can represent more of these features explicitly and provide greater detail about how they develop and interact. This offers opportunities to improve the simulation of regional climate and extreme events, and to investigate processes that are difficult to represent in lower-resolution models.

Higher resolution does not, by itself, guarantee more accurate simulations. Model biases, the representation of physical processes and interactions between the atmosphere and ocean remain important. HRCM research investigates these challenges alongside the benefits of increasing model resolution.

Explore our [research themes](/research/research-themes/) for introductions to some of the climate processes and scientific questions underpinning this work.

#### Research themes

HRCM research addresses a range of questions about the climate system, including:

- **Tropical cyclones and storms:** understanding how high-resolution models represent storm structure, intensity and behaviour.
- **Precipitation and extreme events:** investigating intense rainfall, its regional characteristics and the processes that drive extremes.
- **Atmospheric circulation:** examining large-scale circulation patterns and how they interact with smaller-scale weather systems.
- **Ocean–atmosphere interactions:** understanding how the ocean and atmosphere influence one another and contribute to climate variability.
- **Regional climate and future change:** assessing how climate processes and extreme events may respond to a changing climate.

Together, these themes help evaluate the capabilities and limitations of high-resolution climate models and the information they can provide about future climate.

#### HRCM research and model development

HRCM builds on a history of collaborative work using global climate models at increasingly high resolutions. Research has included the **HiGEM** and **UPSCALE** projects, contributions to **PRIMAVERA**, and work associated with **HighResMIP**, part of the sixth phase of the Coupled Model Intercomparison Project (CMIP6).

These activities have helped develop and evaluate high-resolution modelling approaches, investigate the representation of important climate processes and support international comparisons of climate simulations.

The programme continues to contribute to research on high-resolution and kilometre-scale global climate modelling.

Find out more about the programme's [research projects](/research/projects/) and browse the resulting [publications](/publications/).

#### Our partners

HRCM brings together expertise in climate science, atmospheric and ocean modelling, and the development and analysis of global climate simulations.

The collaboration includes:

**National Centre for Atmospheric Science (NCAS)**

NCAS is a UK research centre that supports atmospheric science through research, facilities and scientific expertise. Its work contributes to understanding the atmosphere and its role in the Earth system.

**University of Reading**

The University of Reading contributes research expertise in climate science, atmospheric processes and climate modelling. Its involvement supports the development and scientific analysis of high-resolution climate simulations.

**Met Office**

The Met Office is the UK's national meteorological service. Its Hadley Centre conducts climate research and develops climate models used to investigate climate variability and change, and to support climate prediction and projections.
