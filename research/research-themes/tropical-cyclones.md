<h3>Tropical cyclones (TCs) are the most damaging meteorological phenomenon.</h3>

<p>Understanding their drivers, impacts and role in the large-scale climate system are major research challenges, and a primary motivation for our research. Typical climate models are run at resolutions that are too coarse to credibly represent tropical cyclones, but the current generation of high-resolution GCMs, including kilometre-scale models, with grid spacings of around 10 km, are able to resolve the main features of these storms. Increased resolution also leads to realistic global statistics and behaviour.</p>

<p><img src="{{ site.baseurl }}/assets/media/uploads/Research_themes_figures/tropical_cyclone.png"></p>
<p><strong><em>Figure | 3D structure of a warm-core tropical cyclone</em></strong></p>

<p>HRCM's TC research focusses on determining how TC simulation is sensitive to resolutions and how models treat convection, and also how models can complement observational records in regions of lower data quality, particularly across the Southern Hemisphere. We also work closely with the private sector, particularly the insurance industry, to explore ways to integrate information derived from simulated TC activity into better catastrophe-risk assessment.</p>
